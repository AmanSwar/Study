An SDE is the continuous-time analogue of an ODE, but with a Brownian driver. This module constructs solutions, classifies them, studies their properties, and connects them to parabolic PDEs.

## 1. What is an SDE?

A **stochastic differential equation (SDE)** is an equation of the form
$$
dX_t = \mu(t, X_t) dt + \sigma(t, X_t)\, dB_t, \qquad X_0 = \xi,
$$
with given coefficients $\mu : [0, T] \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ and $\sigma : [0, T] \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^{d \times m}$, driving Brownian motion $B$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$, and initial condition $\xi$ (random or deterministic).

**Meaning.** A solution is an $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued adapted continuous process $X$ satisfying
$$
X_t = \xi + \int_0^t \mu(s, X_s) ds + \int_0^t \sigma(s, X_s)\, dB_s \qquad \text{a.s., } \forall t \in [0, T].
$$

The integrals are the Lebesgue integral (for the drift) and the Itô integral (for the diffusion).

---

## 2. Strong vs weak solutions

**Definition 2.1 (Strong solution).** Given a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ carrying a Brownian motion $B$ and a filtration $\mathbb{F}$ to which $B$ is adapted, a **strong solution** is an $\mathbb{F}$-adapted process $X$ satisfying the SDE pathwise.

**Definition 2.2 (Weak solution).** A **weak solution** is a triple $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P, X, B)$ such that $B$ is a Brownian motion, $X$ satisfies the SDE, but now we don't require $X$ to be adapted to the filtration generated by $B$ — only the joint law of $(X, B)$ matters.

**Equivalence.** For Lipschitz coefficients, strong and weak solutions coincide; for coefficients with only measurability (e.g., bang-bang drift $\mu(x) = \text{sgn}(x)$), weak solutions can exist even when strong do not — this is the content of the **Stroock–Varadhan martingale problem** and Yamada–Watanabe's theorem.

We focus on strong solutions under Lipschitz conditions, the most relevant case for finance.

## 3. Existence and uniqueness — the classical Itô theorem

**Theorem 3.1 (Itô 1946, Doob).** Suppose $\mu : [0, T] \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ and $\sigma : [0, T] \times \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^{d \times m}$ are measurable, satisfy

- **Lipschitz:** $|\mu(t, x) - \mu(t, y)| + |\sigma(t, x) - \sigma(t, y)| \le K|x - y|$ for all $t, x, y$,
- **Linear growth:** $|\mu(t, x)| + |\sigma(t, x)| \le K(1 + |x|)$ for all $t, x$,

with some constant $K > 0$, and $\xi \in L^2(\mathcal{F}_0)$. Then there exists a unique continuous $\mathbb{F}$-adapted process $X = (X_t)_{0 \le t \le T}$ satisfying the SDE, with $E[\sup_{t \le T} |X_t|^2] < \infty$.

*Proof (Picard iteration).*

Define the iteration in $\mathcal{H} := \{\text{continuous adapted processes with } \|X\|^2 := E[\sup_{t \le T} |X_t|^2] < \infty\}$, which is a Banach space under $\|\cdot\|$.

**Step 1.** Set $X^{(0)}_t := \xi$. Define
$$
X^{(n+1)}_t := \xi + \int_0^t \mu(s, X^{(n)}_s) ds + \int_0^t \sigma(s, X^{(n)}_s) dB_s.
$$

**Step 2.** Linear growth implies each $X^{(n)} \in \mathcal{H}$. In particular, by linear growth,
$$
E\Bigl[\int_0^T |\sigma(s, X^{(n)}_s)|^2 ds\Bigr] \le K^2 T(1 + E[\sup|X^{(n)}|^2]) < \infty,
$$
so the Itô integral is well-defined.

**Step 3 (Contraction).** Using Jensen, Doob's $L^2$ inequality, Itô isometry, and the Lipschitz condition:

$$
E\Bigl[\sup_{s \le t}|X^{(n+1)}_s - X^{(n)}_s|^2\Bigr] \le C(t)\, E\Bigl[\int_0^t |X^{(n)}_s - X^{(n-1)}_s|^2 ds\Bigr],
$$

with $C(t) = 2T K^2 + 8K^2 = C$ (for $t \le T$). Iterating this bound,
$$
\|X^{(n+1)} - X^{(n)}\|^2 \le \frac{(Ct)^n}{n!} \|X^{(1)} - X^{(0)}\|^2 \to 0.
$$

**Step 4 (Cauchy completion).** The partial sums $X^{(n)}$ are Cauchy in $\mathcal{H}$; by completeness, they converge to some $X$ in $\mathcal{H}$. By Itô isometry / dominated convergence, $X$ satisfies the SDE.

**Step 5 (Uniqueness).** If $Y$ is another solution, the same estimate with $X$ and $Y$ in place of successive Picard iterates gives
$$
E\Bigl[\sup_{s \le t}|X_s - Y_s|^2\Bigr] \le C\int_0^t E\Bigl[\sup_{u \le s}|X_u - Y_u|^2\Bigr] ds.
$$
Gronwall's inequality yields $E[\sup|X - Y|^2] = 0$, so $X = Y$ a.s. (as continuous processes, pathwise equal). $\square$

### 3.1 Gronwall's inequality

**Lemma 3.2 (Gronwall, integral form).** If $f(t) \le a + b\int_0^t f(s) ds$ on $[0, T]$ with $a, b \ge 0$ constants and $f \ge 0$ integrable, then $f(t) \le a e^{bt}$.

*Proof.* Set $g(t) = e^{-bt}\int_0^t f$. Then $g'(t) = e^{-bt}(f - b\int f) \le a e^{-bt}$. Integrating $g(t) \le -a (e^{-bt} - 1)/b$. Rearranging gives $\int_0^t f \le a(e^{bt} - 1)/b$, so $f(t) \le a e^{bt}$. $\square$

## 4. Moment bounds and regularity

**Proposition 4.1 (Moment bound).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.1, for any $p \ge 2$,
$$
E\Bigl[\sup_{t \le T}|X_t|^p\Bigr] \le C_{p, T, K}\, (1 + E[|\xi|^p]).
$$

*Proof.* Apply Itô to $|X_t|^p$, then BDG (Module 3.7) to bound the martingale part by the quadratic-variation integral, then Gronwall. Technical but routine — see Karatzas–Shreve Chapter 5.2. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (Continuity of paths).** $X$ has continuous paths a.s.; in fact, $t \mapsto X_t$ is Hölder continuous of exponent $< 1/2$ on $[0, T]$ (Kolmogorov–Chentsov applied to the Itô process).

## 5. Comparison theorems and positivity

**Theorem 5.1 (Scalar comparison).** Let $dX^i_t = \mu_i(t, X^i_t) dt + \sigma(t, X^i_t) dB_t$ for $i = 1, 2$, with same diffusion coefficient $\sigma$, Lipschitz in space, and drifts satisfying $\mu_1 \le \mu_2$. If $X^1_0 \le X^2_0$ a.s., then $X^1_t \le X^2_t$ for all $t$ a.s.

*Proof idea.* Let $Y_t = X^2_t - X^1_t$. Then $dY_t = [\mu_2(t, X^2) - \mu_1(t, X^1)] dt + [\sigma(t, X^2) - \sigma(t, X^1)] dB_t$. The diffusion coefficient is Lipschitz, the drift has a sign condition: when $Y_t = 0$, $\mu_2(t, X^2) - \mu_1(t, X^1) = \mu_2(t, X^1) - \mu_1(t, X^1) \ge 0$. Itô applied to $(Y_t^-)^2$ plus an analysis of the local time at $0$ proves $Y^- \equiv 0$. Full proof: Ikeda–Watanabe, or Karatzas–Shreve Proposition 5.2.18. $\square$

### 5.1 Applications to finance

- **CIR positivity.** For $dX_t = \kappa(\theta - X_t) dt + \sigma\sqrt{X_t}\, dB_t$, comparison with the ODE $dy = \kappa(\theta - y) dt$ shows $X$ stays positive when $2\kappa\theta \ge \sigma^2$ (the **Feller condition**). In general, $X = 0$ is a reflecting or absorbing boundary depending on parameters.
- **Monotonicity of option prices.** If $g_1 \le g_2$ (payoffs), then $E[g_1(X_T)|X_t = x] \le E[g_2(X_T)|X_t = x]$. Comparison formalizes "a call with higher strike has lower price".
- **Bounded interest rates.** In Vasicek / HW, if $|r_0|$ is bounded, comparison with deterministic ODEs shows $|r_t|$ grows at most polynomially.

## 6. Markov property and transition density

**Theorem 6.1 (Markov property).** Under Lipschitz hypotheses, the solution $X$ is a Markov process with respect to its natural filtration. For $t > s$ and bounded measurable $g$,
$$
E[g(X_t)|\mathcal{F}_s] = E[g(X_t)|X_s] =: P_{s, t} g(X_s),
$$
where $P_{s, t}$ is the **transition semigroup**.

*Sketch.* Follows from time-shift invariance of the Brownian increments and uniqueness: conditionally on $X_s = x$, the evolution $s' \mapsto X_{s + s'}$ has the same law as the solution starting at $x$. See Module 3.6 for the full semigroup theory.

### 6.1 Transition density via Kolmogorov

**Theorem 6.2 (Kolmogorov equations).** Let $X$ be the strong solution of the SDE, and let $p(s, x; t, y)$ be the transition density (when it exists) — i.e., $P(X_t \in dy | X_s = x) = p(s, x; t, y) dy$.

Then $p$ satisfies:

**Kolmogorov backward equation** (in $(s, x)$, $s < t$ with $y, t$ fixed):
$$
-\partial_s p = \mu(s, x) \partial_x p + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2(s, x) \partial_{xx} p,
$$
with terminal condition $p(t, x; t, y) = \delta_y(x)$.

**Fokker–Planck / Kolmogorov forward equation** (in $(t, y)$, $t > s$ with $x, s$ fixed):
$$
\partial_t p = -\partial_y(\mu(t, y) p) + \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{yy}(\sigma^2(t, y) p),
$$

with initial condition $p(s, x; s, y) = \delta_x(y)$.

*Sketch.* The backward equation comes from Itô applied to $p(s, X_s; t, y)$ as a function of $(s, x)$; the martingale property gives the PDE. The forward equation is the formal adjoint — see Øksendal §8.1.

### 6.2 Explicit densities

- **Brownian motion:** $p(s, x; t, y) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}\exp(-(y-x)^2/(2(t-s)))$.
- **Geometric BM:** lognormal density.
- **OU:** Gaussian with mean $xe^{-\alpha(t-s)}$ and variance $\sigma^2(1-e^{-2\alpha(t-s)})/(2\alpha)$.
- **CIR:** non-central chi-squared transition density.

## 7. Feynman–Kac revisited

We saw the Feynman–Kac formula in Module 3.3. Here we state and prove a slightly more general version.

**Theorem 7.1 (Feynman–Kac).** Let $X$ solve the SDE with Lipschitz coefficients. Suppose $u(t, x) \in C^{1,2}([0,T] \times \mathbb{R}^d)$ with polynomial growth solves
$$
\partial_t u + \mathcal{L} u - r u + f = 0, \qquad u(T, x) = g(x),
$$
where $\mathcal{L}$ is the infinitesimal generator of $X$ and $r, f, g$ are bounded measurable. Then

$$
u(t, x) = E\Bigl[\int_t^T e^{-\int_t^s r(X_u) du} f(s, X_s) ds + e^{-\int_t^T r(X_u) du} g(X_T)\Big|\, X_t = x\Bigr].
$$

*Proof.* Apply Itô's formula to $M_s := e^{-\int_t^s r(X_u) du} u(s, X_s) + \int_t^s e^{-\int_t^v r du} f(v, X_v) dv$. The PDE implies the drift of $M$ is zero, so $M$ is a local martingale. Under growth conditions (polynomial growth of $u$, $r$, etc.), $M$ is a true martingale. Take expectations of $M_T$ to get the formula. $\square$

### 7.1 Extensions

- **Killing and running payoffs:** combines the discounted terminal payoff with a running cash flow.
- **Boundary conditions:** For SDEs on bounded domains, add a Dirichlet condition on $\partial D$, giving a first-exit-time formula.
- **American options:** Optimal stopping produces a variational inequality / obstacle problem (Module S04 once we reach optimal control).

### 7.2 A killed OU example

Consider the equation
$$
\partial_t u - \alpha x \partial_x u + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \partial_{xx} u = 0, \qquad u(T, x) = x^2.
$$

Feynman–Kac: $u(t, x) = E[X_T^2 | X_t = x]$ with $X_T = xe^{-\alpha(T-t)} + \sigma\int_t^T e^{-\alpha(T-s)} dB_s$. Hence
$$
u(t, x) = x^2 e^{-2\alpha(T-t)} + \frac{\sigma^2}{2\alpha}(1 - e^{-2\alpha(T-t)}).
$$

This can be verified by plugging into the PDE — an elegant cross-check of probability and PDE.

## 8. SDEs in finance: workhorse models

### 8.1 Black–Scholes / GBM

$$
dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dB_t, \qquad S_0 = s.
$$

Lipschitz in log-space: $\log S$ has constant coefficients. Closed form: $S_t = s e^{(\mu - \sigma^2/2)t + \sigma B_t}$.

### 8.2 Vasicek / Hull–White

$$
dr_t = \kappa(\theta - r_t) dt + \sigma dB_t.
$$

Closed form as OU:
$$
r_t = r_0 e^{-\kappa t} + \theta(1 - e^{-\kappa t}) + \sigma\int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t-s)} dB_s.
$$

### 8.3 CIR (Cox–Ingersoll–Ross)

$$
dr_t = \kappa(\theta - r_t) dt + \sigma\sqrt{r_t}\, dB_t.
$$

Coefficient $\sigma\sqrt{x}$ is Hölder-$1/2$ — **not Lipschitz** — so Itô's standard theorem doesn't apply. Existence and uniqueness follow from Yamada–Watanabe's pathwise uniqueness theorem for $1/2$-Hölder diffusions with Lipschitz drift (Yamada–Watanabe 1971).

### 8.4 Heston SV

$$
dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sqrt{V_t} S_t dB^{(1)}_t, \qquad dV_t = \kappa(\theta - V_t) dt + \xi\sqrt{V_t}\, dB^{(2)}_t,
$$
with correlation $\rho$ between $B^{(1)}, B^{(2)}$. Pricing via Fourier inversion (Module 2.5, Carr–Madan).

### 8.5 Local vol (Dupire)

$$
dS_t = rS_t dt + \sigma_{\text{loc}}(t, S_t) S_t\, dB_t,
$$
with $\sigma_{\text{loc}}$ calibrated to market option prices by the Dupire formula
$$
\sigma_{\text{loc}}^2(T, K) = \frac{\partial_T C + rK \partial_K C}{\tfrac{1}{2}K^2 \partial_{KK} C}.
$$

### 8.6 SABR

$$
dF_t = \alpha_t F_t^\beta dB^{(1)}_t, \qquad d\alpha_t = \nu \alpha_t dB^{(2)}_t, \qquad d\langle B^{(1)}, B^{(2)}\rangle = \rho dt.
$$

Used for implied volatility smile modeling in rates and FX.

### 8.7 Rough volatility

$$
dV_t = \text{(non-Markovian driver)}, \qquad V_t \propto \int_0^t (t - s)^{H - 1/2} dW_s, \quad H \in (0, 1/2).
$$

Uses fractional Brownian-like driver; requires rough-path calculus. Empirical $H \approx 0.1$ in real equity volatility data.

## 9. Numerical methods

### 9.1 Euler–Maruyama

Discretize $t_k = k\Delta t$, $\Delta t = T/n$:
$$
X_{k+1} = X_k + \mu(t_k, X_k) \Delta t + \sigma(t_k, X_k)\sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z_k, \qquad Z_k \sim N(0, 1)\text{ iid}.
$$

**Strong convergence** (pathwise): $E[\sup_k |X_k - X_{t_k}|^2]^{1/2} = O(\sqrt{\Delta t})$.

**Weak convergence** (distributional / moments): $|E[g(X_n)] - E[g(X_T)]| = O(\Delta t)$ under smoothness.

### 9.2 Milstein scheme

Improves strong order to $\Delta t$:
$$
X_{k+1} = X_k + \mu \Delta t + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} Z_k + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma \sigma'(X_k)\bigl((\sqrt{\Delta t} Z_k)^2 - \Delta t\bigr).
$$

The additional term is the Itô correction applied to $\sigma$.

### 9.3 Higher-order schemes

- **Runge–Kutta for SDEs** (stochastic RK): match higher weak orders.
- **Multilevel Monte Carlo** (Giles): combine coarse and fine grids to reduce variance of MC estimators.

## 10. Python: simulating SDEs

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
T, n, M = 1.0, 1_000, 50_000
dt = T/n
times = np.linspace(0, T, n+1)

# ---- (a) Euler-Maruyama for GBM ----
S0, mu, sigma = 100.0, 0.05, 0.2
S = np.zeros((M, n+1)); S[:, 0] = S0
dW = rng.standard_normal(size=(M, n)) * np.sqrt(dt)
for k in range(n):
    S[:, k+1] = S[:, k] + mu * S[:, k] * dt + sigma * S[:, k] * dW[:, k]

# Compare with exact
W_T = dW.sum(axis=1)
S_exact = S0 * np.exp((mu - 0.5*sigma**2)*T + sigma * W_T)
strong_err = np.sqrt(((S[:, -1] - S_exact)**2).mean())
print("GBM Euler strong error at T:", strong_err)  # ~ sqrt(dt) ~ 0.03 (rough)

# ---- (b) Vasicek closed form vs Euler ----
kappa, theta, sigma_v, r0 = 1.5, 0.04, 0.02, 0.05
r = np.zeros((M, n+1)); r[:, 0] = r0
for k in range(n):
    r[:, k+1] = r[:, k] + kappa*(theta - r[:, k])*dt + sigma_v * dW[:, k]

# Exact: r_t = r_0 e^{-kT} + theta(1-e^{-kT}) + sigma * integral
# We compare moments
mean_euler = r[:, -1].mean()
var_euler  = r[:, -1].var()
mean_ex    = r0*np.exp(-kappa*T) + theta*(1 - np.exp(-kappa*T))
var_ex     = sigma_v**2 / (2*kappa) * (1 - np.exp(-2*kappa*T))
print(f"Vasicek mean: euler={mean_euler:.5f}, exact={mean_ex:.5f}")
print(f"Vasicek var : euler={var_euler:.6f}, exact={var_ex:.6f}")

# ---- (c) CIR simulation (note: Euler can go negative!) ----
kappa_c, theta_c, xi_c, V0 = 2.0, 0.04, 0.3, 0.04
V_euler = np.zeros((M, n+1)); V_euler[:, 0] = V0
for k in range(n):
    V_pos = np.maximum(V_euler[:, k], 0.0)
    V_euler[:, k+1] = V_euler[:, k] + kappa_c*(theta_c - V_euler[:, k])*dt + xi_c*np.sqrt(V_pos)*dW[:, k]
print("CIR Euler min:", V_euler[:, -1].min())
print("CIR Euler mean:", V_euler[:, -1].mean(), "theory:", theta_c)
# For accurate CIR, use full truncation / Ninomiya-Victoir / exact scheme

# ---- (d) Milstein for GBM ----
S_mil = np.zeros((M, n+1)); S_mil[:, 0] = S0
sigma_prime_S = sigma  # for GBM, sigma(S) = sigma*S, sigma'(S)*sigma(S) = sigma^2 * S
for k in range(n):
    S_mil[:, k+1] = (S_mil[:, k]
                     + mu * S_mil[:, k] * dt
                     + sigma * S_mil[:, k] * dW[:, k]
                     + 0.5 * sigma**2 * S_mil[:, k] * (dW[:, k]**2 - dt))

strong_err_mil = np.sqrt(((S_mil[:, -1] - S_exact)**2).mean())
print("GBM Milstein strong error at T:", strong_err_mil)  # ~ dt, much better

# ---- (e) Feynman-Kac: heat equation via BM ----
# Solve u_t + 1/2 u_xx = 0, u(T, x) = x^2
# Analytic u(0, x0) = x0^2 + T
x0 = 0.5
u_mc = ((x0 + np.sqrt(T) * rng.standard_normal(M))**2).mean()
u_analytic = x0**2 + T
print(f"\nHeat eqn F-K: u_MC={u_mc:.5f}, u_analytic={u_analytic:.5f}")
```

Expected results:

- GBM Euler strong error ~0.03 (so $\sqrt{dt}$ scaling).
- Vasicek moments match to 4 decimals.
- CIR Euler may go negative (famous bug); careful schemes (Alfonsi, exact) fix this.
- GBM Milstein strong error ~0.001, an order of magnitude better.
- Feynman–Kac heat equation recovers $x^2 + T$.

## 11. [QUANT APPLICATION] — SDEs in the trading desk

### 11.1 Calibration of local vol

Given market option prices $C(T, K)$, Dupire's formula computes the local vol $\sigma_{\text{loc}}(T, K)$. The SDE $dS = r S dt + \sigma_{\text{loc}}(t, S) S dB$ recovers exactly the market's marginal distributions at every maturity — this is called **perfect calibration**. Existence-uniqueness theorems guarantee a solution exists (under regularity of the call price surface).

### 11.2 Hedging error under model misspecification

If true dynamics are Heston but you hedge with BS delta:
$$
dP_t^{\text{BS-hedge}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma_{BS}(V_t^{\text{real}} - \sigma_{BS}^2) S_t^2\, dt,
$$

plus a stochastic term from the vega mismatch. The structure of this error is governed by Itô's formula applied to the BS price under Heston dynamics — an application of Itô + SDE existence.

### 11.3 Credit modeling: Merton's structural model

Firm assets follow $dV_t = \mu V_t dt + \sigma V_t dB_t$ (GBM). Equity $E_t$ = European call on $V$ with strike = debt $D$. Default time = first passage of $V$ below $D$. This uses SDEs, Itô, and first-passage theory (Module 3.6).

### 11.4 Risk-neutral pricing as a linear PDE

Every European payoff price $C(t, S) = E^Q[e^{-r(T-t)} g(S_T)|S_t = S]$ solves a linear PDE (Black–Scholes–Merton style). This is Feynman–Kac. American options produce a variational inequality — a free-boundary problem. Quant developers solve these PDEs via finite differences (explicit, implicit, Crank–Nicolson); convergence is via consistency + stability + Lax equivalence.

### 11.5 Greeks via adjoint PDE

The derivative of $V = E^Q[e^{-rT} g(S_T)]$ with respect to a parameter $\theta$ can be computed by solving an adjoint PDE (dual to the pricing PDE) — a well-developed computational technique when pathwise Monte Carlo has high variance.

## 12. Worked examples

### 12.1 Solving the Vasicek SDE explicitly

$dr_t = \kappa(\theta - r_t) dt + \sigma dB_t$. Apply Itô to $Y_t = e^{\kappa t} r_t$:
$$
dY_t = \kappa e^{\kappa t} r_t dt + e^{\kappa t} dr_t = \kappa e^{\kappa t} r_t dt + e^{\kappa t}(\kappa\theta - \kappa r_t) dt + \sigma e^{\kappa t} dB_t = \kappa\theta e^{\kappa t} dt + \sigma e^{\kappa t} dB_t.
$$

Integrating: $e^{\kappa t} r_t = r_0 + \theta(e^{\kappa t} - 1) + \sigma\int_0^t e^{\kappa s} dB_s$.

$$
r_t = r_0 e^{-\kappa t} + \theta(1 - e^{-\kappa t}) + \sigma\int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t-s)} dB_s.
$$

The integral is Gaussian with variance $\sigma^2(1-e^{-2\kappa t})/(2\kappa)$.

### 12.2 Stationary distribution of OU

As $t \to \infty$, $r_t$ converges in distribution to $N(\theta, \sigma^2/(2\kappa))$. This is the **invariant / stationary distribution** of the OU process, a special case of the Fokker–Planck steady state.

### 12.3 Asian option SDE

$dA_t = S_t dt$, with $S$ = GBM. The pair $(S, A)$ is a 2D diffusion:
$$
d\begin{pmatrix}S\\A\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}rS\\S\end{pmatrix} dt + \begin{pmatrix}\sigma S\\0\end{pmatrix} dB.
$$

The Asian call price $C(t, S, A) = E[e^{-r(T-t)}((A_T/T) - K)^+|S_t = S, A_t = A]$ satisfies a 2D PDE — computationally heavier, but well-defined.

### 12.4 Hull–White extended Vasicek

$dr_t = (\theta(t) - \kappa r_t) dt + \sigma dB_t$, with $\theta(t)$ chosen to match the initial yield curve. This generalizes Vasicek with a time-dependent drift; Itô + explicit solution gives the fit to market zero-coupon bond prices.

## 13. Summary

- **SDEs** generalize ODEs to include Brownian driving noise.
- **Existence and uniqueness** under Lipschitz + linear growth via Picard iteration and Gronwall.
- **Comparison theorems** establish monotonicity of solutions in drift.
- **Markov property** holds; transitions governed by **Kolmogorov backward / Fokker–Planck forward equations**.
- **Feynman–Kac** relates SDE functionals to parabolic PDEs.
- **Numerical schemes**: Euler–Maruyama (strong order $1/2$), Milstein (strong order $1$), multilevel Monte Carlo.
- **Finance SDEs**: GBM, Vasicek, CIR, Heston, local vol, SABR, rough vol — all special cases requiring special treatment.

**14.4** Verify that the SDE $dX_t = \text{sgn}(X_t) dB_t$, $X_0 = 0$, **does not** have a strong solution (Tsirelson example). [Hint: show that a strong solution would force $X$ to equal $\pm B$ and contradict the filtration structure.]

**14.5** Use Itô to show the SDE $dX_t = X_t dB_t$ has solution $X_t = X_0 \exp(B_t - t/2)$ — the stochastic exponential.

**★★ (core techniques)**

**14.6** **(Gronwall's inequality, differential form.)** If $\phi(t) \le a + b\int_0^t \phi(s) ds$, prove $\phi(t) \le a e^{bt}$ by defining $\psi(t) = e^{-bt}\int_0^t \phi(s) ds$ and integrating.

**14.7** **(Lipschitz stability.)** Let $X$ and $\bar X$ solve the SDE with the same $\sigma$ but drifts $\mu$ and $\bar\mu$, both Lipschitz. Bound $E[\sup|X_t - \bar X_t|^2]$ in terms of $\|\mu - \bar\mu\|_\infty$.

**14.8** **(Growth bound.)** Prove the $L^p$-bound in Proposition 4.1 for $p = 2$: $E[\sup|X_t|^2] \le C(1 + E[|\xi|^2])$.

**14.9** **(CIR Feller condition.)** For $dX_t = \kappa(\theta - X_t)dt + \sigma\sqrt{X_t} dB_t$ with $X_0 > 0$, show that $P(X_t > 0\,\forall t) = 1$ iff $2\kappa\theta \ge \sigma^2$.

**14.10** **(Comparison.)** Show that if $\mu_1 \le \mu_2$ and $X^1_0 \le X^2_0$, then $X^1 \le X^2$ pathwise a.s. (the Scalar comparison theorem). [Outline Section 5.]

**14.11** **(Vasicek bond price.)** Compute $P(0, T) = E[\exp(-\int_0^T r_s ds)]$ for Vasicek using the fact that $\int_0^T r_s ds$ is Gaussian. Derive the closed form $P(0, T) = \exp(A(T) - B(T) r_0)$ for affine functions $A, B$.

**14.12** **(Euler–Maruyama strong convergence.)** Prove that Euler–Maruyama has strong order $1/2$: $E[\sup|X^{\text{EM}} - X|^2]^{1/2} = O(\sqrt{\Delta t})$. [Hint: use local error + Gronwall.]

**14.13** **(Milstein scheme rigor.)** Derive the Milstein scheme from a Taylor expansion of $X_{t_{k+1}} - X_{t_k}$ keeping terms through $(\sqrt{\Delta t})^2$.

**14.14** **(Kolmogorov backward for Brownian motion.)** For $u(s, x) = E[g(B_T)|B_s = x]$ with $g$ smooth, show $u$ satisfies $\partial_s u + \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{xx} u = 0$.

**14.15** **(Fokker–Planck for OU.)** Derive the stationary density of OU from the Fokker–Planck PDE and verify it is $N(\theta, \sigma^2/(2\kappa))$.

**★★★ (research-level / quant)**

**14.16** **(Yamada–Watanabe.)** Read Yamada–Watanabe (1971). Apply their pathwise uniqueness criterion to show uniqueness of the CIR SDE.

**14.17** **(Heston SDE and Fourier pricing.)** Derive the Heston PDE from Itô on $\log S_t$ and apply Feynman–Kac. Compute the characteristic function of $\log S_T$ via Riccati ODEs.

**14.18** **(Local vol Dupire formula.)** Starting from the BS PDE on $C(t, S)$ under local vol dynamics, differentiate twice in $K$ and once in $T$ (interpreting $C$ as a function of $K, T$) to derive the Dupire formula.

**14.19** **(SDE calibration via MCMC.)** Given noisy observations of a Vasicek process at discrete times, design a Bayesian MCMC (Module 2.7) to estimate $(\kappa, \theta, \sigma)$.

**14.21** **(Jump diffusion.)** Add a compound Poisson jump term: $dX = \mu dt + \sigma dB + dJ_t$. Derive the extended Kolmogorov backward equation with an integro-term; this is the **PIDE** (Module 3.5).
